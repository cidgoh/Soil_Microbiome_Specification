# Scope

## What this specification covers

This specification focuses on contextual data for **terrestrial soil microbiome samples** across agricultural, forest, grassland, and urban environments. It covers the information needed to describe a sample, the conditions under which it was collected, and the processing steps applied before sequencing.

For a full description of the contextual data domains, see [domains.md](domains.md).

## In scope and out of scope

| In scope | Outside current scope |
|---|---|
| Terrestrial soil microbiome sample context | Sediments and aquatic depositional contexts |
| Agricultural, forest, grassland, and urban terrestrial settings | Analytical or derived microbiome data, such as bioinformatic outputs, statistical analyses, or derived ecological indicators |
| Collection details: location, depth, timing, methods, and devices | Wastewater specifications intended for sediment or wastewater contexts |

## Boundary notes

**Soil vs. sediment**: The distinction is based on environmental setting and sampling context, not solely on the material's composition. A terrestrial soil sample from a site near a water body remains in scope when the setting is terrestrial in character. Aquatic sediments are outside the current boundary and may be addressed through existing wastewater specifications. Edge cases should be documented and flagged for review.

**Sample context vs. analytical data**: This specification covers information collected at or before the point of sampling. It does not cover what is learned from the sample through analysis. Sequencing outputs, abundance tables, diversity indices, and derived indicators are out of scope.

**Scope does not imply uniformity**: Being in scope does not mean every project must collect the same variables or follow the same local workflow. Projects can adopt the specification in full, in part, or use it only as a reference.

## Broader relevance

Literature review has identified potential applications beyond the defined scope, including urban environmental work and forensics. These examples illustrate the broader relevance of contextual data harmonization but do not change the current scope of the specification.
